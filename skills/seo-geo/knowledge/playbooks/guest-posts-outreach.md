# Guest posts and outreach playbook

## In plain words
Earned backlinks and bylined mentions on other people's sites are slower and
more effort-intensive than directories or social posts, but they carry more
weight — a link or mention from a site with its own audience and authority
signals both search engines and AI assistants that independent third parties
vouch for the brand, which is exactly the "talked about across many sources"
pattern that drives being recommended rather than just found.

## What practitioners need to know

### Target selection
- **"Best X" listicles that already exist in the category are the highest
  -leverage target.** If a comparison/listicle article already ranks and
  gets cited for the exact category the brand competes in, getting added to
  an existing, already-trusted page is usually faster and higher-value than
  getting a brand-new page published from scratch. Many listicle authors are
  open to adding a genuinely relevant entry if asked directly and
  specifically.
- **Journalist/blogger query platforms** (the HARO-style category: sites
  that route journalist and blogger source requests) are a source of
  low-effort, high-authority bylined mentions — answering a relevant query
  with a specific, useful quote earns a mention with a link, for the cost of
  writing one good answer. HARO itself was discontinued/relaunched under
  Cision; as of 2026 the useful current platforms to name are **Qwoted**,
  **Featured** (the closest spiritual successor — structured questions,
  picked answers published across a network of sites), **MentionMatch**
  (formerly Help A B2B Writer, now under Superpath — most relevant for a B2B
  SaaS brand specifically), and **Source of Sources** (founded by HARO's
  original founder, has a free tier) [industry, converged across several
  2026 sources, no primary platform comparison found]. Best practice:
  **respond within the first hour**, **lead with a ready-to-publish quote**
  rather than a pitch to be interviewed later, and **give specific,
  original expertise rather than generic AI-sounding answers** — editors are
  now explicitly filtering out AI-sounding responses, a dynamic that didn't
  exist when HARO was the only game in town [industry].
- **Prioritize sites whose existing content already covers adjacent topics**,
  not just sites with high general authority. A relevant but smaller site is
  usually a better fit — and a more likely "yes" — than a generic
  high-authority site with no topical connection. This matters more than it
  might look: most inbound "accepts guest posts" sites are low quality by
  default — one analysis of ~26K sites found **73% of webmasters receive
  guest-post requests daily and reject more than 90%** for low quality, and
  only about **7.6% of guest-post-accepting sites meet a baseline quality
  bar (DR 65+, 10K+ traffic)** [industry, vendor-reported dataset, not
  independently audited]. A templated pitch is competing against dozens of
  other templated pitches for a small pool of genuinely good targets — which
  is exactly why the specific, topically-relevant pitch below outperforms a
  generic one.
- **A genuinely useful free tool or resource is a link magnet worth pitching
  on its own.** If the product includes something usable without a
  purchase (a free analysis, calculator, or audit), pitching that
  specifically — rather than the product generally — to newsletters and
  topical communities tends to earn more natural, unprompted links, because
  it's something people want to reference regardless of whether they
  become a customer.

### Pitch structure
Personalization has a large, repeatedly-reported effect on response rate:
one source citing a large-scale outreach study puts personalized guest-post
pitches at roughly **9.8% acceptance on DA 70+ sites vs 1.2% for generic
pitches**, and a separate cold-email study found **11.7% success when
personalized to the specific publication**, with one vendor's own campaign
data (1,000 emails, four campaigns) landing a **20.5% response rate**
*(industry/heuristic — the "9.8% vs 1.2%" figure is attributed to a named
study that couldn't be independently located and traced only through
secondary sources, so treat that specific number as heuristic; the
cold-email and vendor-campaign figures are industry-tier, self-reported by
the platforms running them)*. Directionally, all of it corroborates the same
point below:
1. **Reference the specific existing page or query, not a generic
   introduction.** A pitch that shows the sender actually read the target
   listicle/query and explains precisely why the addition fits gets a much
   higher response rate than a templated "we'd love to be featured"
   message.
2. **Lead with the specific value to their reader, not the product's
   features.** State the concrete thing their audience gains (a genuinely
   different angle, a data point their current list is missing, a category
   their comparison hasn't covered) before describing the product itself.
3. **Make the ask small and specific**: "would you consider adding one line
   with this link" is an easier yes than "would you write about us" or "can
   we guest post." The lower the effort being asked of the target, the
   higher the response rate.
4. **Include exactly what they'd need to act — a one-line description, the
   URL, and (for listicles) where it fits relative to existing entries —
   so accepting the pitch requires no back-and-forth.**
5. **Follow up once, not repeatedly.** A single, polite follow-up after a
   reasonable gap is normal; repeated unsolicited follow-ups read as
   pressure and damage the relationship for any future outreach to the same
   target. Follow-ups do measurably help — one source reports **follow-ups
   lift reply rates 20–30%**, and most replies to a campaign arrive only
   after at least one follow-up [industry, vendor-attributed] — but that
   data doesn't say repeated follow-ups keep helping; the "once" guidance
   here is about relationship management, not proven reply-rate
   maximization *(heuristic beyond the first-follow-up figure)*.

### Pacing and tracking
Treat this as an ongoing, low-volume weekly activity (a handful of targeted
pitches per week) rather than a one-time blast — response rates are
naturally low, so consistency over time outperforms a single large batch.
Track the same fields as directory submissions (target, date, status,
resulting link/mention URL, notes) so pitches aren't duplicated and pending
follow-ups aren't lost.
